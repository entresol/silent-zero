# ABI Research 2026: ranking of real-time operating systems for robotics safety

ABI Research. "QNX, Wind River, SYSGO, and Green Hills Software Named Leaders in ABI Research's Commercial Real-Time Operating System (RTOS) for Robotics Functional Safety Ranking", press release of 22 April 2026. [Page](https://www.abiresearch.com/press/qnx-wind-river-sysgo-and-green-hills-software-named-leaders-in-abi-researchs-commercial-real-time-operating-system-rtos-for-robotics-functional-safety-ranking). Read in full. A press release about an analyst report, part of the firm's research service; the ranking itself was not read.

## What it shows

- **A market category with named leaders.** The firm names QNX, Wind River, SYSGO and Green Hills Software as leaders in a ranking of commercial RTOS for robotics functional safety. The subtitle says vendors "with certified architectures, mixed-criticality support, and deep ecosystem partnerships are gaining a competitive edge".
- **Reasons given.** QNX led for its microkernel architecture, extensive safety certifications and ecosystem; Wind River had the top implementation score, with its VxWorks installed base; SYSGO's PikeOS stood out for its separation-kernel and hypervisor approach to mixed-criticality robotics ([[pikeos]]); Green Hills was recognised for a high-assurance, security-first architecture.
- **Trends the analyst names.** Stricter functional-safety standards, AI-ready robotics platforms, demand for edge-to-cloud safety integration, wider adoption of mixed-criticality architectures with deterministic scheduling and hypervisor support, and a convergence of functional safety and cybersecurity.

## How

An analyst's judgement summarised in a press release. No method, scores or sample.

## Limits

The ranking, its criteria and scores were not read. An analyst's ranking is evidence that a category and a market exist, not of the quality of any product. The release says nothing about formal verification.

## Against the wiki

- [[safety-path]]: **extends**. Our reading: the category is the commercial counterpart of the paper's split.

## For zOS

It places zOS beside an established category of certified RTOS vendors. It names no open-source or formally verified entrant; our reading: such a kernel is not part of the ranked category, which does not tell whether buyers would consider it. See [[qnx]], [[pikeos]].
