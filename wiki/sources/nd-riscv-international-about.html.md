# RISC-V International: About

> Who keeps the RISC-V standard: a Swiss non-profit; royalty-free ISA, no implementations. report, full text.

Source · LLM reviewed · updated 2026-10-31 · https://silentzero.ooo/wiki/sources/nd-riscv-international-about.html

The "About" page of RISC-V International, undated, read on 31 October 2026. [Page](https://riscv.org/about/).

## What it shows

- **Who keeps the standard.** RISC-V International calls itself "the global non-profit home of the open standard RISC-V Instruction Set Architecture (ISA), related specifications, and stakeholder community". It was founded in 2015 as the RISC-V Foundation, with 36 founding members, and incorporated in Switzerland as RISC-V International Association in March 2020.
- **What "open" means here.** The ISA and the extensions it ratifies are "royalty free" and provided under open licences; "anyone may leverage RISC-V as a building block in their open or proprietary solutions and services". RISC-V International "does not manage or make available any open RISC-V implementations, only the standard specifications".
- **Origins.** Krste Asanović, Yunsup Lee and Andrew Waterman started the ISA in May 2010 in Berkeley's Parallel Computing Laboratory, which David Patterson directed. The first publication is the 2011 report ([The RISC-V Instruction Set Manual, Version 1.0](https://silentzero.ooo/wiki/sources/2011-waterman-risc-v-instruction.html.md)); the 2014 paper on open instruction sets ([Instruction Sets Should Be Free](https://silentzero.ooo/wiki/sources/2014-asanovic-instruction-sets-free.html.md)) is named as a milestone.
- **Licences and patents.** The ISA encoding was "effectively put into the public domain" with the technical reports; the text of the specification was later put under a Creative Commons licence. "No patents were filed related to RISC-V" in these projects.
- **Why Switzerland.** Concerns about continued access to the IP, raised in 2018 and 2019; the page says the move "does not circumvent any existing restrictions".
- **Funding.** The original ISA work was not federally funded; later DARPA programmes used RISC-V; RISC-V International "has never had DARPA funding".

## Limits

The organisation describes itself. A careful reader adds: "royalty-free" covers the ISA, not the cores or chips that implement it, as the page itself says.
