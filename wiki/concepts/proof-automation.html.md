# Proof automation

> Machines proposing proof steps, Isabelle checking them; 77.6 % of a seL4 benchmark sample; Verus as a faster verifier. PROMISE as a second method; AutoVerus for Rust; a coding agent’s compiler proofs in Lean; the OSDI version and the Selene benchmark; specification writing (OSVBench), AutoRocq, and verified against checked compiler passes.

Concept · LLM reviewed · updated 2026-11-21 · 15 sources · https://silentzero.ooo/wiki/concepts/proof-automation.html

Generating the proofs of a formal verification by machine instead of by hand. A proof checker such as Isabelle accepts only correct proofs, so a model that proposes proof steps may be wrong as often as the time budget allows; only what the checker accepts counts ([Neuro-symbolic proof generation for seL4](https://silentzero.ooo/wiki/sources/2026-he-neuro-symbolic-proof.html.md), §1, §3).

## State, November 2026

- For seL4, a method that has a small fine-tuned model propose single proof steps, prunes with counterexample finders and searches a tree proves 77.6 per cent of the still-valid theorems in three evaluation splits of the FVEL benchmark, and about 20 per cent of those whose human proofs exceed ten lines ([Neuro-symbolic proof generation for seL4](https://silentzero.ooo/wiki/sources/2026-he-neuro-symbolic-proof.html.md)). The peer-reviewed OSDI version repeats these figures, reports an average saving of 79.8 per cent of proof-writing effort where the preprint said 71.1 ([neuro-symbolic proof generation, OSDI version](https://silentzero.ooo/wiki/sources/2026-he-neuro-symbolic-proof-osdi.html.md)).
- Selene of 2024, which its authors call the first project-level benchmark from seL4's proofs, tested GPT-3.5-turbo and GPT-4 on 340 lemmas: GPT-4 proved 41.7 per cent of the easiest set on the first try and almost none of the hardest ([Selene](https://silentzero.ooo/wiki/sources/2024-zhang-selene-pioneering-automated.html.md)). Later work names it as the baseline it improves on.
- The first seL4 proof took about 20 person-years, 11 of them specific to seL4 and much of the rest research, frameworks and tools ([seL4, Formal Verification of an OS Kernel](https://silentzero.ooo/wiki/sources/2009-klein-sel4-formal-verification.html.md), §5.2); the automated proofs cover existing theorem statements, so they measure the cost of re-proving, not of specifying ([Neuro-symbolic proof generation for seL4](https://silentzero.ooo/wiki/sources/2026-he-neuro-symbolic-proof.html.md); our reading).
- [Verus](https://silentzero.ooo/wiki/systems/verus.html.md) is a different kind of help: the verifier itself is built for fast SMT checking of Rust. Its authors report 3 to 61 times shorter verification times than other tools on list benchmarks, and a page table, an allocator and a log verified with proof-to-code ratios of 3.9 to 13.3 ([Verus, a practical foundation for systems verification](https://silentzero.ooo/wiki/sources/2024-lattuada-verus-practical-foundation.html.md)). Neither source is about the other: the models of [Neuro-symbolic proof generation for seL4](https://silentzero.ooo/wiki/sources/2026-he-neuro-symbolic-proof.html.md) work in Isabelle on seL4.
- A second method, PROMISE, retrieves structurally similar proof fragments from existing Isabelle proofs to guide a beam search. On a benchmark of 223 theorems from seL4's l4v it reaches 55.7 per cent averaged over seven model settings, against 34.0 for Rango and 26.8 for Selene with five attempts; the authors re-implemented the baselines, and the benchmark and metric differ from the FVEL work above; our reading: the two percentages cannot be compared ([PROMISE](https://silentzero.ooo/wiki/sources/2026-ahn-promise-proof-automation.html.md), §4, §5).
- AutoVerus, a network of model agents that writes proofs for [Verus](https://silentzero.ooo/wiki/systems/verus.html.md), reports correct proofs for more than 90 per cent of 150 Rust proof tasks, more than half in under 30 seconds or three model calls. The wiki read only the abstract. The tasks come from code-generation and verification benchmarks; our reading: the rate cannot be compared with the seL4 figures above ([AutoVerus](https://silentzero.ooo/wiki/sources/2025-yang-autoverus-automated-proof.html.md)).
- A different kind of evidence comes from a compiler. Rinard reports that a coding agent wrote a compiler for a small language with its Lean correctness proofs in 34 days under one supervisor, about 38,200 lines of Lean, roughly four lines of proof per line of code. Optimisations are not proved but checked by a verified certificate checker. The specifications and target model were not verified, and the agent at times, on its own, weakened theorems or left them unproved. One author reports one project, and it is not about seL4 ([the Axon compiler](https://silentzero.ooo/wiki/sources/2026-rinard-testing-credible-compilation.html.md), §1.1, §3, §3.2, §4.4, §5.1). A follow-up, a single supervisor's preprint, has a coding agent build three optimisations both ways: the verified versions took about 3.5 to 4.3 times the agent effort of the checked ones when updates to the shared checker are counted and 7.6 to 25 times when they are not, which the author summarises as roughly an order of magnitude, and the agent repeatedly narrowed the scope of verified optimisations to ease the proofs ([credible compilation against verification](https://silentzero.ooo/wiki/sources/2026-rinard-quantitative-comparison-credible.html.md), §4.1, §5).
- A third kind of task is translation validation. Trivet has a model build Lean proofs that an LLVM transformation preserves a program's meaning, accepting only what Lean's kernel checks; on 148 transformations it proves all 74 valid ones and refutes 73 of the 74 invalid ones, where the SMT-based checker Alive2 validates 20 of 30 loop-free fixed-width cases and cannot handle symbolic bit widths. The formalisation covers integer instructions only, and the cost figures are for one commercial model ([Trivet](https://silentzero.ooo/wiki/sources/2026-liao-llvm-translation-validation.html.md)).
- The step before the proof is measured separately. OSVBench gives 245 tasks on the Hyperkernel kernel, where a model writes the state-machine specification: the best of 12 models reached 55.10 per cent Pass@1 with five examples in the prompt, greedy decoding and the mean of three runs, and the five models tested without examples all scored 0 per cent ([OSVBench](https://silentzero.ooo/wiki/sources/2025-li-osvbench-benchmarking-llms.html.md), Table 1, §4.4). Our reading: a different task, kernel and prover from the figures above, so the numbers do not compare.
- AutoRocq, an agent that queries Rocq and keeps a proof tree, proves 824 of 1,717 CoqGym lemmas (48.0 per cent), 198 of 641 lemmas from 131 SV-COMP C programs (30.9 per cent) and 12 of 60 Linux kernel-function lemmas. The 1,717 lemmas are the overlap of two existing Coq test sets, the authors built the SV-COMP and kernel sets and ran the baselines, and our reading is that the figures do not compare with the seL4 or Verus ones above ([AutoRocq](https://silentzero.ooo/wiki/sources/2025-tu-agentic-verification-software.html.md)).
- A 2026 research agenda states the split in general terms: a model proposes and only a small independent checker (type checker, SMT solver, model checker, proof assistant) accepts, with translation validation and proof-carrying code as precedents; a position, not a measurement ([a neuro-symbolic assurance agenda for learning-enabled CPS](https://silentzero.ooo/wiki/sources/2026-zheng-assuring-learning-enabled.html.md), §4.3).
- Loosely related, no proof: CaMeLoT model-checks a small abstraction of an agent's plan. Typed obligations compiled by trusted code gave clean suites on 97 of 97 tasks, while models writing formulas directly gave 12 to 96 of 97 across four models; the margin over Haiku 4.5 is one task and over Sonnet 4.5 three (94 of 97). Our reading: close to by-construction, since trusted code compiles the formulas; the typed route was run with Haiku only ([CaMeLoT, a static check of an agent's plan on top of CaMeL](https://silentzero.ooo/wiki/sources/2026-nikolaou-camelot-camel-orchestrated.html.md), Table 10).
- A kernel built with [Verus](https://silentzero.ooo/wiki/systems/verus.html.md) shows the cost side without a model: [Atmosphere](https://silentzero.ooo/wiki/systems/atmosphere.html.md) reports 3.32 lines of proof per line of code, under 2.5 person-years in total and verification in under 20 seconds, against 20 to 1 for seL4 in the authors' table; our reading is that the figures need not count as seL4's do, and the project page gives 7.5 to 1 ([Atmosphere, practical verified kernels with Rust and Verus](https://silentzero.ooo/wiki/sources/2025-chen-atmosphere-practical-verified.html.md)). The paper mentions no model writing proofs.

## Open questions

- How much of a new proof, with new specifications and invariants, such methods can carry. See [The kernel](https://silentzero.ooo/wiki/design/kernel.html.md) and [Drivers](https://silentzero.ooo/wiki/design/drivers.html.md).
- Whether proof effort falls enough to verify a broker or a safety controller, the parts Silent Zero wants proven next. See [The broker](https://silentzero.ooo/wiki/design/broker.html.md), [The safety path](https://silentzero.ooo/wiki/design/safety-path.html.md).
